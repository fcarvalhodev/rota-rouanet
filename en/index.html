Rouanet Route

Do artists get rich from the Rouanet Law?

Many people think the Rouanet Law, Brazil's main cultural tax-incentive law, pays million-real fees to famous artists. Here is how it works, how much an artist can receive through it and where the real million-real fees come from.

📊 See the summary in icons

The short answer

A project's value is not an artist's personal fee. Ministry of Culture Normative Instruction 29/2026 sets R$25,000 per performance for an individual fee, with exceptions. That cap does not set the total a person can receive across several events. Any claim about millions requires identifying projects and payments. The municipal concerts investigated in 2022 used a different kind of contract.

How Rouanet works

Under the tax-incentive mechanism, companies and individuals can direct part of their income tax to culture. The government gives up revenue; permission to raise funds is not a payment to the artist.

📝

The producer sends the project to the Ministry of Culture, which reviews it.

✅

If approved, the project gets permission to look for sponsorship. It still has no money at all.

🏢

Companies taxed on actual profit and individuals can sponsor it, deducting from their income tax under the legal conditions and limits.

🧾

The project takes place and the producer has to account for what was spent.

That is why “approved” is not “spent”. In 2023, about R$16 billion in projects were approved, but only R$2.3 billion was raised.

The fee cap

How much a solo artist can receive per performance in a Rouanet project:

In February 2022, the Bolsonaro government cut the cap by 93%. In April 2023, the Lula government raised it to R$25,000, still below the R$45,000 of before. The current rule allows additional amounts from non-incentivized funds or a justified request to the CNIC. The cap is per performance, not per year.

Rouanet is not a city hall concert

In 2022, singer Zé Neto criticized artists who use Rouanet. Then million-real fees for sertanejo (Brazilian country music) concerts came to light, and prosecutors began investigating. The case became known as the “Sertanejo CPI”. But the money was different:

🎭

Rouanet Law

  • Sponsorship from companies and individuals with a tax incentive, under the legal conditions.
  • Individual fee: R$25,000 per performance, with exceptions.
  • Project reviewed by the Ministry of Culture.
🏛️

City hall concert

  • Money from the city's own budget.
  • Has no Rouanet fee cap.
  • Hiring without bidding must meet the legal requirements; today, art. 74, II, of Law 14.133/2021.

Comparing the Rouanet cap with fees paid by city halls in 2022:

Prosecutors opened investigations in at least 36 cities. In Teolândia, Bahia, the fee for a single concert was more than 40% of what the city had spent on health the previous year. The problem continues: in 2025, city halls in Minas Gerais spent about R$538 million on concerts, and the state audit court stepped up oversight.

Is it true?

Needs proof

“Famous artists make millions from Rouanet.” There is a cap per performance, with exceptions. The total a project raises is not personal income. Only payment records can verify a specific case.

True

“Bolsonaro lowered the Rouanet fee to R$3,000.” That was in February 2022. Before, it was R$45,000.

True

“Lula raised the Rouanet fee.” It went up to R$25,000 in April 2023. It is still less than before 2022.

False

“The million-real sertanejo concerts were paid for by Rouanet.” They were paid for by city halls, with the city's own money.

False

“Lula spent R$16 billion on Rouanet in 2023.” That is the amount approved. Companies sponsored R$2.3 billion.

Half true

“Rouanet is public money.” The tax incentive is tax the government does not collect from sponsors. It is indirect public support; it is not the same as a direct budget transfer.

Half true

“Rouanet only helps the Southeast.” In 2024, 86 out of every 100 projects were in the South and Southeast. But today there are projects in every state.

False

“There was never any theft in Rouanet.” Operation Boca Livre, in 2016, investigated suspected diversion of about R$21 million; accusation and conviction are distinct stages.

In short

  • The Rouanet incentive lets companies and individuals sponsor culture with part of their taxes.
  • The individual fee has a cap per performance, currently R$25,000, with exceptions. It is not a cap on total earnings.
  • The R$1 million fees came from city halls, not from Rouanet.
  • The law moves the economy, but the money is still concentrated in the South and Southeast.

Other changes

In 2022, the cap for a regular project fell from R$1 million to R$500,000, theater rental was limited to R$10,000 and the deadline to seek sponsorship fell from 36 to 24 months. Culture secretary Mario Frias said the idea was to make the law “fairer and more popular”. In 2023, the cap began to range from R$1 million to R$10 million, four-year plans returned and the culture commission (CNIC) resumed work.

FGV study (2026)

In 2024 the projects moved R$25.7 billion in the economy. The government gave up a little over R$3 billion in taxes and collected R$3.9 billion in taxes from the culture sector. 85.5% of payments went to micro and small businesses. The study was commissioned by the Ministry of Culture.
